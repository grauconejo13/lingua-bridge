# Product Vision

## Problem

People can be highly knowledgeable yet less comfortable expressing ideas in a non-native language. Remote collaboration becomes harder when someone's connection drops words or cuts out. Existing tools may be limited to specific meeting platforms, language combinations, pricing tiers, or connectivity assumptions.

## Vision

Enable meaningful conversations across languages and network conditions, on the devices people already have.

**Working tagline:** Speak naturally. Be understood anywhere.

## Users and situations

1. **Remote teams and professional collaborators:** multilingual conversations during meetings and demos.
2. **Students / educators:** follow technical explanations and ask questions in a preferred language.
3. **In-person pairs:** each person uses a phone and optional earbuds.
4. **Presenters / attendees:** many listeners select languages through a browser/QR join link.
5. **Small organizations:** affordable translation that does not assume premium devices or perfect internet.

## Desired experience

- A speaker talks naturally, with informed consent for audio capture.
- The listener selects their preferred language.
- Captions and/or synthesized translated audio are delivered with understandable latency.
- When the connection weakens, the experience shifts toward lower-bandwidth options and clearly shows delivery status.
- When the network disappears, locally saved messages can be retried later if the device supports it and the user opts in.

## What we are *not* promising yet

- Perfect or instantaneous translation.
- Reliable simultaneous multi-party speech on poor networks.
- Full offline speech-to-speech translation on every device.
- Replacement of Zoom's own meeting audio or universal Zoom plugin compatibility.
- Voice cloning or camera-based interpretation as baseline features.

## Success hypotheses to validate

- People find multilingual captions and translated audio useful enough to adopt.
- A simple browser workflow can outperform cumbersome setup for a chosen use case.
- Message and text fallback helps conversations survive interruptions.
- The service can be offered at an affordable cost without losing money per user.

## Social impact and sustainability

Explore a mission-driven business supporting affordable communication, with potential educational/free usage supported by paid organizations. A nonprofit is an option to examine later, not a prerequisite.

## Research questions

- Which language pairs are underserved for our target users?
- What translation accuracy is acceptable for technical conversations?
- What are current competitors' gaps and total cost of ownership?
- Do users prefer AI voices, original audio plus captions, or both?
- What are consent and privacy expectations in public and private settings?
